## I. TITLE OF THE INVENTION

**Computer-Implemented Method for Evaluating Fidelity, Safety, and Legal Compliance of AI-Generated Character Responses Using Structured Canonical Profile Documents and Multi-Dimensional Large Language Model Adjudication**

---

## II. ABSTRACT

A computer-implemented method for evaluating artificial intelligence (AI) generated text responses that purport to portray a fictional or licensed character. The method comprises: (a) receiving a structured character profile document ("Character Card") containing a canonical facts pack, a voice/personality profile pack, a legal rights and performer consent pack, and a content safety pack; (b) receiving an AI-generated text response produced by any large language model (LLM) in response to a user prompt directed at the character; (c) independently evaluating the AI-generated response across at least four scoring dimensions—canon fidelity, voice consistency, brand safety, and legal compliance—by submitting the response and the corresponding Character Card pack to a separate adjudicating LLM operating as a judge; (d) computing per-dimension numerical scores on a normalized scale; (e) computing a weighted aggregate score using configurable dimension weights; (f) determining a pass/fail certification outcome by comparing each per-dimension score and the aggregate score against configurable threshold values; and (g) storing the evaluation results, scores, explanations, and pass/fail determination in a persistent data store associated with the character, enabling longitudinal quality tracking across models, versions, and time periods. The method is model-agnostic with respect to the AI system that generated the response under evaluation, enabling intellectual property owners to evaluate outputs from any LLM provider against a single canonical character definition.

---

## III. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to computer-implemented methods for quality assurance of AI-generated content, and more specifically to methods for evaluating whether text generated by large language models accurately and safely portrays licensed fictional characters according to canonical, legal, and brand safety specifications defined by intellectual property owners.

## IV. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### A. The Problem

The rapid proliferation of large language models (LLMs) capable of generating conversational text has created unprecedented demand for interactive AI character experiences — chatbots, non-player characters (NPCs) in video games, branded digital assistants, and interactive entertainment. Major intellectual property (IP) owners including film studios, game publishers, toy companies, and consumer brands seek to deploy AI-powered versions of their licensed characters across multiple platforms (messaging apps, game engines, voice assistants, custom applications).

However, current LLMs present fundamental risks to IP owners when generating character-portraying text:

1. **Canon Drift**: LLMs frequently hallucinate facts, invent biographical details, misstate character relationships, and contradict established lore. A model portraying a children's character might fabricate a dark backstory that contradicts decades of canonical material.

2. **Voice Inconsistency**: LLMs may generate text that fails to match a character's established personality, tone, vocabulary, speech patterns, or mannerisms. A character known for simple, childlike speech might be rendered with sophisticated vocabulary that breaks immersion and brand identity.

3. **Brand Safety Violations**: Without per-character guardrails, LLMs may generate content that is off-brand, age-inappropriate, or damaging to the IP owner's reputation. Generic content moderation tools detect toxicity but cannot enforce character-specific content ratings, prohibited topics, or required disclosures.

4. **Legal and Regulatory Non-Compliance**: An emerging and complex web of regulations governs AI-generated content featuring real or fictional characters. These include SAG-AFTRA performer consent requirements for digital replicas, the EU AI Act's transparency mandates, the pending NO FAKES Act establishing federal digital replica rights, and at least 46 U.S. state laws regulating AI-generated media. A character AI system must verify that performer consent is active, that usage falls within consented scope and territories, and that required legal disclosures are present.

5. **No Existing Standard**: There is no established industry framework for certifying that AI character portrayals meet canonical, brand, and legal standards. IP owners currently rely on ad hoc manual review, which is unscalable, inconsistent, and unable to operate at the speed required for real-time interactive experiences.

### B. Limitations of Existing Approaches

**Generic LLM Safety Tools** (e.g., OpenAI Moderation API, content classifiers): These tools detect toxicity, hate speech, and general content policy violations but have no concept of character-specific canon, brand voice, or IP-specific legal constraints. They cannot determine whether a response about "Peppa Pig" accurately reflects the show's established facts.

**Character AI Platforms** (e.g., Character.AI, Inworld AI): These platforms provide AI character conversation capabilities but are primarily user-generated content platforms with no IP licensing infrastructure, no formal evaluation framework, no canonical source of truth governance, and no certification capability. They have faced safety controversies that undermine studio trust.

**General LLM Evaluation Platforms** (e.g., Braintrust, DeepEval, Promptfoo, LangSmith, Arize Phoenix): These platforms provide general-purpose LLM evaluation capabilities including custom rubrics and LLM-as-judge scoring. However, they lack: (i) the specific structured data model for character canonical profiles integrating canon, legal, and safety information; (ii) the specific multi-dimensional scoring methodology designed for character fidelity assessment; (iii) integration of performer consent verification and legal compliance into the evaluation pipeline; and (iv) the character-versioned evaluation history enabling longitudinal tracking of how a specific character's AI portrayal quality changes across models and time.

**Enterprise Content Moderation** (e.g., Hive Moderation, Spectrum Labs): These solutions are reactive (detecting bad content after generation) rather than proactive (ensuring generated content meets affirmative character-specific quality criteria). They have no concept of "canon fidelity" or "voice consistency" for a specific character.

### C. Need for the Invention

There exists a need for a systematic, automated, model-agnostic method for evaluating AI-generated character responses against a structured canonical profile that integrates factual canon, voice/personality specifications, legal rights and performer consent terms, and content safety rules — and that produces multi-dimensional scores enabling IP owners to certify, compare, and continuously monitor the quality of character AI portrayals across any LLM provider.

## V. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a computer-implemented method comprising the following principal steps:

**Step 1 — Character Card Definition:** Creating and storing a structured character profile document ("Character Card") comprising at least three component packs:
- A **Canon Pack** containing a machine-readable database of canonical facts (key-value pairs with source citations), a voice/personality profile (personality traits, tone, vocabulary preferences, speech patterns, catchphrases), and a relationship graph (connections to other characters with relationship types);
- A **Legal Pack** containing rights metadata (IP owner, licensed territories, valid dates), performer consent records (performer identity, consent type, consent date, expiration, territory restrictions, usage restrictions, strike clause status), and required legal notices;
- A **Safety Pack** containing a content rating (e.g., G, PG, PG-13, R), a list of prohibited topics, a list of required disclosures (e.g., "This is an AI-generated experience"), and age-gating configuration.

Each Character Card is **versioned and immutable** once published — modifications create new versions, preserving a complete audit trail. The Character Card is associated with a franchise (an IP collection) and an organization (the IP owner), enabling multi-tenant operation.

**Step 2 — Response Receipt:** Receiving, via an application programming interface (API), a tuple comprising: (i) an identifier of the Character Card against which evaluation is requested; (ii) a user prompt or input that was presented to an AI system portraying the character; and (iii) the AI-generated text response produced by said AI system. Critically, the method does not require knowledge of or access to the AI system that produced the response — it evaluates only the output text.

**Step 3 — Evaluation Context Assembly:** Retrieving the specified Character Card version from a persistent data store and extracting the relevant pack data to construct evaluation context documents for each scoring dimension.

**Step 4 — Multi-Dimensional LLM-as-Judge Evaluation:** Independently evaluating the AI-generated response across at least four dimensions by submitting structured evaluation prompts to a separate adjudicating LLM (the "Judge LLM"). Each evaluation comprises:

- **(a) Canon Fidelity Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the canon facts database, the relationship graph, the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates factual accuracy (does the response align with established facts?), relationship accuracy (are character relationships portrayed correctly?), and lore consistency (does anything contradict established backstory?). The Judge LLM returns a numerical score (0-100) and a natural language explanation.

- **(b) Voice Consistency Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the voice/personality profile (personality traits, tone, speech style, vocabulary level, catchphrases), the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates personality match, tone appropriateness, vocabulary consistency, and speech pattern fidelity. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

- **(c) Brand Safety Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the content rating, the prohibited topics list, the required disclosures list, the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates content rating compliance, prohibited topic avoidance, required disclosure inclusion, and overall brand protection. Any mention of prohibited topics results in a score below a critical threshold. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

- **(d) Legal Compliance Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the rights metadata, the performer consent terms (including consent type, restrictions, and territories), the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates whether usage falls within performer consent scope, whether the response respects territory restrictions and usage limitations, and whether required legal notices are present. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

Each dimension evaluation uses a **structured prompt template** that instructs the Judge LLM to operate as a domain-specific expert evaluator, to score on a 0-100 scale with defined anchor points, and to return results in a parseable format (SCORE: [number] / EXPLANATION: [text]).

**Step 5 — Score Aggregation:** Computing a weighted aggregate total score by applying configurable weights to the per-dimension scores:

```
total_score = (canon_score × W_canon) + (voice_score × W_voice) + (safety_score × W_safety) + (legal_score × W_legal)
```

Where W_canon, W_voice, W_safety, and W_legal are configurable positive real numbers summing to 1.0. In one embodiment, the default weights are: canon fidelity 0.30, voice consistency 0.25, brand safety 0.30, legal compliance 0.15.

**Step 6 — Pass/Fail Determination:** Comparing each per-dimension score against a configurable per-dimension threshold and the aggregate score against an aggregate threshold. The response passes if and only if ALL per-dimension scores meet or exceed their respective thresholds. Failure reasons are enumerated, identifying which dimensions fell below threshold and by how much.

A higher certification level ("CanonSafe Certified") may be awarded when the response passes and the aggregate score exceeds a higher certification threshold (e.g., 85.0).

**Step 7 — Result Persistence and Longitudinal Tracking:** Storing the complete evaluation result — including all per-dimension scores, explanations, pass/fail determination, failure reasons, evaluation latency, and metadata — in a persistent data store linked to the specific Character Card, Character Card version, and (optionally) a test suite and test case. This enables:
- Longitudinal tracking of how a character's AI portrayal quality changes over time
- Cross-model comparison (evaluating the same character prompts against different LLM providers)
- Regression detection (identifying when a model update degrades character portrayal quality)
- Aggregate reporting (pass rates, average scores, dimension-specific weakness identification)

## VI. DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### A. System Architecture

The preferred embodiment comprises a cloud-hosted service (the "Evaluation Service") accessible via a RESTful API, comprising:

1. **API Gateway Layer**: Receives evaluation requests, authenticates callers via JWT tokens, and routes to internal services. All data is scoped to an organization (multi-tenant architecture).

2. **Character Card Service**: Manages CRUD operations on Character Cards and their versioned contents. Each Character Card belongs to a Franchise (an IP collection) which belongs to an Organization. Cards progress through a status workflow: draft → pending_approval → approved → archived.

3. **Evaluation Engine**: The core of the invention. Receives a Character Card identifier, user prompt, and AI-generated response. Assembles evaluation context from the Character Card version. Dispatches four independent evaluation requests to the Judge LLM. Parses structured responses. Computes aggregate score. Determines pass/fail. Returns complete result.

4. **Test Suite Service**: Manages collections of test cases organized by category (canon, voice, safety, refusal, edge_case). Enables batch evaluation runs where a complete test suite is executed against a Character Card.

5. **Data Layer**: PostgreSQL database storing Organizations, Users, Franchises, Character Cards, Card Versions, Test Suites, Test Cases, Evaluation Runs, and Evaluation Results.

### B. Character Card Data Model

A Character Card Version (the immutable evaluation-time snapshot) contains the following structured fields:

**Canon Pack:**
- `canon_facts`: A JSON object mapping fact keys to values with optional source citations. Example: `{"hometown": {"value": "A small house on a hill", "source": "Season 1, Episode 1"}}`
- `canon_voice`: A JSON object containing personality description, tone, speech style, vocabulary level, and catchphrases. Example: `{"personality": "cheerful, bossy", "tone": "enthusiastic", "catchphrases": ["snort!"]}`
- `canon_relationships`: A JSON array of relationship entries, each containing an entity name, relationship type, and optional notes. Example: `[{"entity": "George Pig", "relationship": "younger brother"}]`

**Legal Pack:**
- `legal_rights`: A JSON object containing IP owner, licensed territories, and validity dates.
- `legal_performer_consent`: A JSON object containing performer name, consent type (AI_DIGITAL_REPLICA, VOICE_ONLY, FULL_LIKENESS), consent date, expiration date, territory restrictions, usage restrictions, and strike clause status.

**Safety Pack:**
- `safety_content_rating`: A string value from a defined set (G, PG, PG-13, R).
- `safety_prohibited_topics`: A JSON array of topic strings that must not appear in responses.
- `safety_required_disclosures`: A JSON array of disclosure strings that should appear when contextually relevant.
- `safety_age_gating`: A JSON object specifying whether age verification is required and the minimum age.

### C. Evaluation Method — Detailed Process Flow

#### C.1 Quick Evaluation (Single Response)

1. Caller submits POST request to `/api/v1/evaluations/evaluate` with body: `{character_card_id, prompt, model_response}`
2. System authenticates caller and verifies the Character Card belongs to the caller's organization.
3. System retrieves the Character Card's current version (the most recently published immutable snapshot).
4. System calls `CardVersion.to_dict()` to extract the canon, legal, and safety packs into a unified evaluation context dictionary.
5. System instantiates the EvaluationService and calls `evaluate_single(character_card, card_version, prompt, model_response)`.
6. The EvaluationService sequentially (or in parallel) invokes four private evaluation methods:

   **`_evaluate_canon_fidelity(context, prompt, response)`:**
   - Constructs a system prompt instructing the Judge LLM to act as a "canon fidelity expert evaluator"
   - Constructs a user prompt containing: the formatted canon facts (key-value pairs with sources), the formatted relationships (entity-relationship pairs), the user's original prompt, and the AI response under evaluation
   - Submits to the Judge LLM (e.g., OpenAI gpt-4o-mini at temperature 0.1 for near-deterministic evaluation)
   - Parses the structured response (SCORE: N / EXPLANATION: text)
   - Returns `{score: float, explanation: str}`

   **`_evaluate_voice_consistency(context, prompt, response)`:**
   - System prompt instructs evaluation of "voice consistency — how well the response captures the character's unique voice and personality"
   - User prompt contains: personality, tone, speech style, vocabulary level, catchphrases from the voice profile, plus the original prompt and AI response
   - Same Judge LLM submission and parsing

   **`_evaluate_brand_safety(context, prompt, response)`:**
   - System prompt instructs evaluation of "brand safety — whether the response is appropriate for the brand and target audience"
   - User prompt contains: content rating, prohibited topics list (formatted as bullets), required disclosures list, plus original prompt and AI response
   - System prompt includes the rule: "Any mention of prohibited topics should result in a score below 50"
   - Same Judge LLM submission and parsing

   **`_evaluate_legal_compliance(context, prompt, response)`:**
   - System prompt instructs evaluation of "legal compliance — whether the response respects legal boundaries and performer rights"
   - User prompt contains: rights metadata (owner, territories), performer consent details (performer, consent type, restrictions), plus original prompt and AI response
   - Same Judge LLM submission and parsing

7. System computes weighted aggregate: `total = (canon × 0.30) + (voice × 0.25) + (safety × 0.30) + (legal × 0.15)`
8. System checks each dimension against its threshold (default: canon ≥ 80, voice ≥ 70, safety ≥ 80, legal ≥ 80). Any dimension below threshold generates a failure reason string.
9. Response passes if and only if the failure_reasons list is empty.
10. If passed AND total ≥ 85.0, the response receives "CanonSafe Certified" status.
11. System persists the evaluation as an EvalRun record (with prompt and response stored for history) and an EvalResult record (with all per-dimension scores, explanations, and pass/fail data).
12. System returns the complete result to the caller.

#### C.2 Batch Evaluation (Test Suite Run)

1. Caller submits a request specifying a Character Card, a Test Suite, and model configuration.
2. System retrieves all Test Cases in the specified Test Suite.
3. For each Test Case, the system generates a model response (or accepts a pre-generated response) and runs the full four-dimension evaluation described above.
4. System aggregates results: total tests, passed tests, failed tests, per-dimension averages.
5. System stores the EvalRun with aggregate statistics and individual EvalResults per test case.

#### C.3 Real-Time Guardrail Mode (Future Embodiment)

In a further embodiment, the method operates as a real-time middleware:
1. User message received by application
2. Pre-check: validate user message for safety and prompt injection
3. Route to configured LLM to generate character response
4. Post-check: evaluate generated response against Character Card using the four-dimension method
5. If pass: return response to user
6. If fail: regenerate with stricter prompt parameters, or return a safe fallback response
7. Log complete interaction with evaluation scores for audit

### D. Judge LLM Prompt Engineering

The Judge LLM prompts are designed with the following principles:

1. **Role Specification**: Each prompt begins by establishing the Judge LLM as an "expert evaluator" for the specific dimension, providing domain context.

2. **Explicit Scoring Criteria**: Each prompt enumerates 3-4 specific sub-criteria that the Judge must consider (e.g., for canon: factual accuracy, relationship accuracy, lore consistency, no hallucination).

3. **Anchor Points**: Each prompt defines what scores of 0 and 100 mean for the dimension (e.g., "100 means perfect alignment with all canon facts; 0 means completely contradicts canon").

4. **Critical Rules**: Certain prompts include bright-line rules (e.g., "Any mention of prohibited topics should result in a score below 50") to ensure that clear violations receive appropriately low scores regardless of other factors.

5. **Structured Output Format**: All prompts require the Judge to respond in an exact parseable format: `SCORE: [number 0-100]` followed by `EXPLANATION: [2-3 sentences]`.

6. **Low Temperature**: The Judge LLM operates at temperature 0.1 (near-deterministic) to maximize scoring consistency across repeated evaluations of identical inputs.

### E. Model Agnosticism

A critical feature of the invention is that it is **model-agnostic with respect to the AI system that produced the response under evaluation**. The method receives only the text output and evaluates it against the Character Card. The underlying model (OpenAI GPT-4, Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, Meta Llama, or any other LLM) is irrelevant to the evaluation process. This enables:

1. IP owners to switch between LLM providers without recreating evaluation infrastructure
2. Cross-model comparison (running identical test suites against multiple providers)
3. Future-proofing (new models can be evaluated without system changes)
4. Separation of concerns (the evaluation system has no dependency on any specific LLM vendor)

The Judge LLM itself may be any suitable LLM, and may differ from the model that generated the response under evaluation.

### F. Versioning and Longitudinal Tracking

Character Cards are versioned. Each version is immutable once published. This enables:

1. **Evaluation reproducibility**: A given evaluation can always be re-traced to the exact Character Card version used
2. **Canon evolution tracking**: As a character's canonical facts evolve (e.g., new movie reveals new backstory), new card versions capture the evolution while preserving history
3. **Regression detection**: If a previously-passing model begins failing after a model update, the system identifies the regression because evaluations are linked to specific card versions
4. **Approval workflow**: Card versions progress through draft → pending_approval → approved, with multi-stakeholder review

### G. Taxonomy and Tag System

The preferred embodiment includes a taxonomy system with categories and tags that standardize character metadata across the platform:
- **Prohibited Content** tags with severity levels (e.g., violence:high, profanity:medium)
- **Character Traits** tags (e.g., friendly, loyal, brave)
- **Content Ratings** (G, PG, PG-13, R)
- **Relationship Types** (family, friend, romantic)

This enables consistent tagging across characters and franchises, supporting cross-character analytics and standardized safety enforcement.

## IX. PRIOR ART CONSIDERATIONS AND SEARCH GUIDANCE

This section is provided to assist IP counsel in conducting a thorough prior art search and assessing patentability.

### A. Known Prior Art Categories

**1. LLM-as-Judge / G-Eval:**
- The concept of using one LLM to evaluate another LLM's output is established. Key references include:
  - Liu et al., "G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment" (2023) — Proposes using LLMs with chain-of-thought as evaluators for natural language generation tasks.
  - Zheng et al., "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (2023) — Studies reliability of LLM judges.
- **Our distinction**: G-Eval and general LLM-as-Judge papers address generic NLG evaluation (fluency, coherence, relevance). They do not address character-specific canon fidelity, brand voice consistency, or integration of legal compliance data (performer consent, territory restrictions) into the evaluation pipeline.

**2. LLM Evaluation Platforms:**
- Braintrust, DeepEval, Promptfoo, LangSmith, Arize Phoenix — all provide frameworks for custom LLM evaluation rubrics.
- **Our distinction**: These are general-purpose evaluation tools. None provide: (i) the specific structured "Character Card" data model integrating canon facts, voice profiles, legal consent, and safety rules into a single versioned document; (ii) the specific four-dimension scoring methodology designed for character fidelity; (iii) performer consent verification as an evaluation dimension; or (iv) character-versioned longitudinal quality tracking.

**3. Content Moderation:**
- OpenAI Moderation API, Hive, Spectrum Labs — detect toxicity, hate speech, NSFW content.
- **Our distinction**: Content moderation is a single-dimension binary (safe/unsafe) or toxicity score. Our method evaluates across four character-specific dimensions with weighted aggregation, and includes affirmative quality assessment (does the response sound like the character?) not just negative detection (is the content harmful?).

**4. Character AI Platforms:**
- Character.AI, Inworld AI, Fable — provide AI character conversation.
- **Our distinction**: These platforms generate character responses but do not provide a systematic evaluation framework for scoring generated responses against structured canonical profiles. They generate content; we evaluate content. They are model-specific; we are model-agnostic.

**5. Structured Character Profiles:**
- "Character bibles" used in entertainment production — narrative documents defining character attributes.
- Character.AI character definition fields — basic personality/scenario text.
- **Our distinction**: Prior character definitions are unstructured narrative text or simple text fields. Our Character Card is a machine-readable structured JSON document with discrete packs (canon, legal, safety), each with specific typed fields designed to serve as evaluation criteria. The inclusion of legal compliance data (performer consent, territory restrictions, strike clauses) within the character profile is believed to be novel.

### C. Strongest Novelty Arguments

1. **The Character Card as an integrated evaluation document**: The combination of canon facts + voice profile + performer consent + safety rules in a single versioned machine-readable structure designed specifically as evaluation criteria for AI-generated content is believed to be novel. Prior character definitions are narrative, not structured for automated evaluation.

2. **Performer consent verification as an evaluation dimension**: Including legal compliance (specifically performer consent scope verification per SAG-AFTRA requirements) as a scored dimension alongside content quality metrics in an automated evaluation pipeline is believed to be novel. Prior evaluation systems assess content quality; they do not verify legal compliance of character AI usage.

3. **Model-agnostic character evaluation with longitudinal tracking**: A system that evaluates any LLM's character portrayal against a canonical standard, across multiple scored dimensions, with versioned evaluation history enabling cross-model comparison and regression detection. Prior model evaluation tools are general-purpose and not character-specific.

4. **The specific weighted four-dimension scoring methodology**: Canon fidelity + voice consistency + brand safety + legal compliance as a defined set of evaluation dimensions with configurable weights and per-dimension pass/fail thresholds, specifically designed for character AI quality assurance.

### D. Weakest Points / Potential Challenges

1. **Abstractness under Alice**: Software method patents face scrutiny under Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank (2014). The claims should emphasize the specific technical steps (structured prompt construction, multi-dimensional parallel evaluation, score parsing, weighted aggregation) rather than the abstract idea of "evaluating character quality."

2. **Obviousness over LLM-as-Judge + Character Profiles**: An examiner might argue that combining known LLM-as-Judge techniques with known character profile data is an obvious combination. The strongest counter-argument is that the specific integration of legal compliance (performer consent verification) into the evaluation pipeline, and the specific structured Character Card format designed to serve as machine-readable evaluation criteria, are non-obvious extensions.

3. **Rapidly evolving field**: The AI evaluation space is moving quickly. Patents filed in 2024-2025 by Braintrust, DeepEval, or similar companies may be relevant prior art that is not yet published or searchable.
