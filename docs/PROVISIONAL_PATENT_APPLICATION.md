## SPECIFICATION

### TITLE OF THE INVENTION

Computer-Implemented Method for Evaluating Fidelity, Safety, and Legal Compliance of AI-Generated Character Responses Using Structured Canonical Profile Documents and Multi-Dimensional Large Language Model Adjudication

### CROSS-REFERENCE TO RELATED APPLICATIONS

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to computer-implemented methods for quality assurance of AI-generated content, and more specifically to methods for evaluating whether text generated by large language models accurately and safely portrays licensed fictional characters according to canonical, legal, and brand safety specifications defined by intellectual property owners.

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

#### The Problem

The rapid proliferation of large language models (LLMs) capable of generating conversational text has created unprecedented demand for interactive AI character experiences — chatbots, non-player characters (NPCs) in video games, branded digital assistants, and interactive entertainment. Major intellectual property (IP) owners including film studios, game publishers, toy companies, and consumer brands seek to deploy AI-powered versions of their licensed characters across multiple platforms (messaging apps, game engines, voice assistants, custom applications).

However, current LLMs present fundamental risks to IP owners when generating character-portraying text:

1. **Canon Drift**: LLMs frequently hallucinate facts, invent biographical details, misstate character relationships, and contradict established lore. A model portraying a children's character might fabricate a dark backstory that contradicts decades of canonical material.

2. **Voice Inconsistency**: LLMs may generate text that fails to match a character's established personality, tone, vocabulary, speech patterns, or mannerisms. A character known for simple, childlike speech might be rendered with sophisticated vocabulary that breaks immersion and brand identity.

3. **Brand Safety Violations**: Without per-character guardrails, LLMs may generate content that is off-brand, age-inappropriate, or damaging to the IP owner's reputation. Generic content moderation tools detect toxicity but cannot enforce character-specific content ratings, prohibited topics, or required disclosures.

4. **Legal and Regulatory Non-Compliance**: An emerging and complex web of regulations governs AI-generated content featuring real or fictional characters. These include SAG-AFTRA performer consent requirements for digital replicas, the EU AI Act's transparency mandates, the pending NO FAKES Act establishing federal digital replica rights, and numerous U.S. state laws regulating AI-generated media. A character AI system must verify that performer consent is active, that usage falls within consented scope and territories, and that required legal disclosures are present.

5. **No Existing Standard**: There is no established industry framework for certifying that AI character portrayals meet canonical, brand, and legal standards. IP owners currently rely on ad hoc manual review, which is unscalable, inconsistent, and unable to operate at the speed required for real-time interactive experiences.

#### Limitations of Existing Approaches

**Generic LLM Safety Tools** (e.g., OpenAI Moderation API, content classifiers): These tools detect toxicity, hate speech, and general content policy violations but have no concept of character-specific canon, brand voice, or IP-specific legal constraints. They cannot determine whether a response about a particular fictional character accurately reflects the character's established facts.

**Character AI Platforms** (e.g., Character.AI, Inworld AI): These platforms provide AI character conversation capabilities but are primarily user-generated content platforms with no IP licensing infrastructure, no formal evaluation framework, no canonical source of truth governance, and no certification capability.

**General LLM Evaluation Platforms** (e.g., Braintrust, DeepEval, Promptfoo, LangSmith, Arize Phoenix): These platforms provide general-purpose LLM evaluation capabilities including custom rubrics and LLM-as-judge scoring. However, they lack: (i) the specific structured data model for character canonical profiles integrating canon, legal, and safety information; (ii) the specific multi-dimensional scoring methodology designed for character fidelity assessment; (iii) integration of performer consent verification and legal compliance into the evaluation pipeline; and (iv) the character-versioned evaluation history enabling longitudinal tracking of how a specific character's AI portrayal quality changes across models and time.

**Enterprise Content Moderation** (e.g., Hive Moderation, Spectrum Labs): These solutions are reactive (detecting bad content after generation) rather than proactive (ensuring generated content meets affirmative character-specific quality criteria). They have no concept of "canon fidelity" or "voice consistency" for a specific character.

#### Need for the Invention

There exists a need for a systematic, automated, model-agnostic method for evaluating AI-generated character responses against a structured canonical profile that integrates factual canon, voice/personality specifications, legal rights and performer consent terms, and content safety rules — and that produces multi-dimensional scores enabling IP owners to certify, compare, and continuously monitor the quality of character AI portrayals across any LLM provider.

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a computer-implemented method comprising the following principal steps:

**Step 1 — Character Card Definition:** Creating and storing a structured character profile document ("Character Card") comprising at least three component packs:
- A **Canon Pack** containing a machine-readable database of canonical facts (key-value pairs with source citations), a voice/personality profile (personality traits, tone, vocabulary preferences, speech patterns, catchphrases), and a relationship graph (connections to other characters with relationship types);
- A **Legal Pack** containing rights metadata (IP owner, licensed territories, valid dates), performer consent records (performer identity, consent type, consent date, expiration, territory restrictions, usage restrictions, strike clause status), and required legal notices;
- A **Safety Pack** containing a content rating (e.g., G, PG, PG-13, R), a list of prohibited topics, a list of required disclosures (e.g., "This is an AI-generated experience"), and age-gating configuration.

Each Character Card is versioned and immutable once published — modifications create new versions, preserving a complete audit trail. The Character Card is associated with a franchise (an IP collection) and an organization (the IP owner), enabling multi-tenant operation.

**Step 2 — Response Receipt:** Receiving, via an application programming interface (API), a tuple comprising: (i) an identifier of the Character Card against which evaluation is requested; (ii) a user prompt or input that was presented to an AI system portraying the character; and (iii) the AI-generated text response produced by said AI system. Critically, the method does not require knowledge of or access to the AI system that produced the response — it evaluates only the output text.

**Step 3 — Evaluation Context Assembly:** Retrieving the specified Character Card version from a persistent data store and extracting the relevant pack data to construct evaluation context documents for each scoring dimension.

**Step 4 — Multi-Dimensional LLM-as-Judge Evaluation:** Independently evaluating the AI-generated response across at least four dimensions by submitting structured evaluation prompts to a separate adjudicating LLM (the "Judge LLM"). Each evaluation comprises:

- **(a) Canon Fidelity Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the canon facts database, the relationship graph, the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates factual accuracy (does the response align with established facts?), relationship accuracy (are character relationships portrayed correctly?), and lore consistency (does anything contradict established backstory?). The Judge LLM returns a numerical score (0-100) and a natural language explanation.

- **(b) Voice Consistency Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the voice/personality profile (personality traits, tone, speech style, vocabulary level, catchphrases), the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates personality match, tone appropriateness, vocabulary consistency, and speech pattern fidelity. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

- **(c) Brand Safety Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the content rating, the prohibited topics list, the required disclosures list, the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates content rating compliance, prohibited topic avoidance, required disclosure inclusion, and overall brand protection. Any mention of prohibited topics results in a score below a critical threshold. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

- **(d) Legal Compliance Evaluation:** The Judge LLM receives the rights metadata, the performer consent terms (including consent type, restrictions, and territories), the user prompt, and the AI response. It evaluates whether usage falls within performer consent scope, whether the response respects territory restrictions and usage limitations, and whether required legal notices are present. The Judge LLM returns a score (0-100) and explanation.

Each dimension evaluation uses a structured prompt template that instructs the Judge LLM to operate as a domain-specific expert evaluator, to score on a 0-100 scale with defined anchor points, and to return results in a parseable format.

**Step 5 — Score Aggregation:** Computing a weighted aggregate total score by applying configurable weights to the per-dimension scores:

    total_score = (canon_score × W_canon) + (voice_score × W_voice) + (safety_score × W_safety) + (legal_score × W_legal)

Where W_canon, W_voice, W_safety, and W_legal are configurable positive real numbers summing to 1.0. In one embodiment, the default weights are: canon fidelity 0.30, voice consistency 0.25, brand safety 0.30, legal compliance 0.15.

**Step 6 — Pass/Fail Determination:** Comparing each per-dimension score against a configurable per-dimension threshold and the aggregate score against an aggregate threshold. The response passes if and only if ALL per-dimension scores meet or exceed their respective thresholds. Failure reasons are enumerated, identifying which dimensions fell below threshold and by how much.

A higher certification level may be awarded when the response passes and the aggregate score exceeds a higher certification threshold (e.g., 85.0).

**Step 7 — Result Persistence and Longitudinal Tracking:** Storing the complete evaluation result — including all per-dimension scores, explanations, pass/fail determination, failure reasons, evaluation latency, and metadata — in a persistent data store linked to the specific Character Card, Character Card version, and (optionally) a test suite and test case. This enables:
- Longitudinal tracking of how a character's AI portrayal quality changes over time
- Cross-model comparison (evaluating the same character prompts against different LLM providers)
- Regression detection (identifying when a model update degrades character portrayal quality)
- Aggregate reporting (pass rates, average scores, dimension-specific weakness identification)

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

#### A. System Architecture

The preferred embodiment comprises a cloud-hosted service (the "Evaluation Service") accessible via a RESTful API, comprising:

1. **API Gateway Layer**: Receives evaluation requests, authenticates callers via JWT tokens, and routes to internal services. All data is scoped to an organization (multi-tenant architecture).

2. **Character Card Service**: Manages CRUD operations on Character Cards and their versioned contents. Each Character Card belongs to a Franchise (an IP collection) which belongs to an Organization. Cards progress through a status workflow: draft → pending_approval → approved → archived.

3. **Evaluation Engine**: The core of the invention. Receives a Character Card identifier, user prompt, and AI-generated response. Assembles evaluation context from the Character Card version. Dispatches four independent evaluation requests to the Judge LLM. Parses structured responses. Computes aggregate score. Determines pass/fail. Returns complete result.

4. **Test Suite Service**: Manages collections of test cases organized by category (canon, voice, safety, refusal, edge_case). Enables batch evaluation runs where a complete test suite is executed against a Character Card.

5. **Data Layer**: Relational database storing Organizations, Users, Franchises, Character Cards, Card Versions, Test Suites, Test Cases, Evaluation Runs, and Evaluation Results.

Referring now to FIG. 1, the system architecture is shown schematically. Client applications (100) communicate with the API Gateway (110) which authenticates requests and routes them to the Character Card Service (120), the Evaluation Engine (130), or the Test Suite Service (140). The Evaluation Engine (130) communicates with the LLM Adapter Layer (150) to submit evaluation prompts to the Judge LLM. All services read from and write to the Data Layer (160).

#### B. Character Card Data Model

Referring now to FIG. 2, a Character Card Version (the immutable evaluation-time snapshot) is shown with the following structured fields:

**Canon Pack (200):**
- `canon_facts` (210): A JSON object mapping fact keys to values with optional source citations. Example: `{"hometown": {"value": "A small house on a hill", "source": "Season 1, Episode 1"}, "favorite_activity": {"value": "jumping in muddy puddles", "source": "Season 1, Episode 1"}}`
- `canon_voice` (220): A JSON object containing personality description, tone, speech style, vocabulary level, and catchphrases. Example: `{"personality": "cheerful, bossy, confident", "tone": "enthusiastic, matter-of-fact", "speech_style": "simple sentences, direct statements", "vocabulary_level": "age 4-6", "catchphrases": ["snort!", "silly daddy"]}`
- `canon_relationships` (230): A JSON array of relationship entries, each containing an entity name, relationship type, and optional notes. Example: `[{"entity": "George Pig", "relationship": "younger brother", "notes": "Peppa is sometimes bossy with George but loves him"}, {"entity": "Mummy Pig", "relationship": "mother"}]`

**Legal Pack (240):**
- `legal_rights` (250): A JSON object containing IP owner, licensed territories, and validity dates. Example: `{"owner": "Hasbro/Entertainment One", "territories": ["worldwide"], "valid_from": "2024-01-01", "valid_to": "2026-12-31"}`
- `legal_performer_consent` (260): A JSON object containing performer name, consent type (AI_DIGITAL_REPLICA, VOICE_ONLY, FULL_LIKENESS), consent date, expiration date, territory restrictions, usage restrictions, and strike clause status. Example: `{"performer": "Harley Bird", "consent_type": "VOICE_ONLY", "consent_date": "2024-06-01", "territories": ["US", "UK", "EU"], "restrictions": ["no_political_content", "no_alcohol_endorsement"], "strike_clause": false}`

**Safety Pack (270):**
- `safety_content_rating` (280): A string value from a defined set: G, PG, PG-13, R. Example: `"G"`
- `safety_prohibited_topics` (282): A JSON array of topic strings that must not appear in responses. Example: `["violence", "death", "romantic_relationships", "politics", "religion", "alcohol", "drugs", "scary_content"]`
- `safety_required_disclosures` (284): A JSON array of disclosure strings that should appear when contextually relevant. Example: `["This is an AI-generated character experience", "Not affiliated with the show's production"]`
- `safety_age_gating` (286): A JSON object specifying whether age verification is required and the minimum age. Example: `{"enabled": false, "minimum_age": 0, "verification_required": false}`

Each Character Card is uniquely associated with:
- A **Franchise** (300): An IP collection (e.g., "Peppa Pig," "Star Wars") owned by an Organization
- An **Organization** (310): The IP owner (e.g., a studio, publisher, or brand)
- One or more **Card Versions** (320): Immutable snapshots, each with an incrementing version number

#### C. Evaluation Method — Detailed Process Flow

##### C.1 Quick Evaluation (Single Response)

Referring now to FIG. 3, the evaluation pipeline is shown as a flow diagram:

1. A caller submits (400) a POST request to the evaluation API endpoint with a body containing: `{character_card_id, prompt, model_response}`, where `character_card_id` identifies the Character Card, `prompt` is the user input that was presented to the AI system, and `model_response` is the AI-generated text to be evaluated.

2. The system authenticates (410) the caller via JWT token and verifies that the Character Card belongs to the caller's organization.

3. The system retrieves (420) the Character Card's current version — the most recently published immutable snapshot containing the full three-pack structure.

4. The system extracts (430) the canon, legal, and safety packs from the Character Card version into a unified evaluation context dictionary.

5. The system dispatches (440) four independent evaluation requests to the Judge LLM. In the preferred embodiment, these evaluations may be performed sequentially or in parallel:

   **Canon Fidelity Evaluation (441):**
   - A system prompt is constructed instructing the Judge LLM to act as a "canon fidelity expert evaluator"
   - A user prompt is constructed containing: the formatted canon facts as key-value pairs with source citations, the formatted character relationships as entity-relationship pairs, the user's original prompt, and the AI response under evaluation
   - The evaluation prompt is submitted to the Judge LLM (in one embodiment, OpenAI GPT-4o-mini at temperature 0.1 for near-deterministic evaluation, though any suitable LLM may be used)
   - The structured response is parsed to extract a numerical score (0-100) and a natural language explanation
   - The method returns a result containing `{score: float, explanation: str}`

   **Voice Consistency Evaluation (442):**
   - A system prompt instructs the Judge LLM to evaluate "voice consistency — how well the response captures the character's unique voice and personality"
   - A user prompt is constructed containing: personality description, tone specification, speech style, vocabulary level, and catchphrases from the voice profile, plus the original prompt and AI response
   - The same Judge LLM submission and parsing procedure is followed
   - The method returns a result containing `{score: float, explanation: str}`

   **Brand Safety Evaluation (443):**
   - A system prompt instructs the Judge LLM to evaluate "brand safety — whether the response is appropriate for the brand and target audience"
   - A user prompt is constructed containing: the content rating, the prohibited topics list formatted as bullet points, the required disclosures list, plus the original prompt and AI response
   - The system prompt includes the rule: "Any mention of prohibited topics should result in a score below 50" — this ensures that clear brand safety violations receive appropriately low scores regardless of other factors
   - The same Judge LLM submission and parsing procedure is followed
   - The method returns a result containing `{score: float, explanation: str}`

   **Legal Compliance Evaluation (444):**
   - A system prompt instructs the Judge LLM to evaluate "legal compliance — whether the response respects legal boundaries and performer rights"
   - A user prompt is constructed containing: rights metadata (IP owner, territories, validity dates), performer consent details (performer name, consent type, territory restrictions, usage restrictions), plus the original prompt and AI response
   - The same Judge LLM submission and parsing procedure is followed
   - The method returns a result containing `{score: float, explanation: str}`

6. The system computes (450) a weighted aggregate score:

    total_score = (canon_score × W_canon) + (voice_score × W_voice) + (safety_score × W_safety) + (legal_score × W_legal)

   In the preferred embodiment, default weights are: W_canon = 0.30, W_voice = 0.25, W_safety = 0.30, W_legal = 0.15. These weights are configurable by the organization to reflect their priorities (e.g., a children's entertainment company might increase W_safety).

7. The system checks (460) each per-dimension score against its configurable threshold. In the preferred embodiment, default thresholds are: canon fidelity ≥ 80, voice consistency ≥ 70, brand safety ≥ 80, legal compliance ≥ 80. Any dimension scoring below its threshold generates a failure reason string identifying the dimension and the deficit.

8. The system determines (470) pass/fail: the response passes if and only if the failure_reasons list is empty (all dimensions met their thresholds).

9. If the response passed AND the aggregate total_score exceeds a certification threshold (default: 85.0), the response receives (480) an elevated certification status (in one embodiment, "CanonSafe Certified").

10. The system persists (490) the complete evaluation as:
    - An **EvalRun** record containing: the character card ID, character card version ID, the prompt, the model response, the total weighted score, the pass/fail determination, failure reasons (if any), evaluation latency, and metadata
    - An **EvalResult** record for each dimension containing: the dimension name, the score, the weight applied, the natural language explanation, and the pass/fail status for that dimension

11. The system returns (495) the complete result to the caller, including all per-dimension scores, explanations, the aggregate score, pass/fail status, failure reasons, and certification status.

##### C.2 Batch Evaluation (Test Suite Run)

Referring now to FIG. 5, the batch evaluation flow is shown:

1. A caller submits a request specifying a Character Card identifier, a Test Suite identifier, and optionally model configuration parameters.

2. The system retrieves the Test Suite and its associated Test Cases. Each Test Case is categorized (e.g., canon, voice, safety, refusal, edge_case) and contains a prompt and an expected behavior description.

3. For each Test Case, the system executes the full four-dimension evaluation described in Section C.1 above.

4. The system aggregates results: total tests executed, tests passed, tests failed, per-dimension average scores, per-category pass rates.

5. The system stores the EvalRun with aggregate statistics and individual EvalResults per test case, enabling drill-down analysis.

##### C.3 Real-Time Guardrail Mode (Further Embodiment)

In a further embodiment, the method operates as a real-time middleware interceptor in a character AI conversation pipeline:

1. A user message is received (500) by the character AI application.

2. **Pre-check** (510): The user message is validated for safety and prompt injection attempts. Messages attempting to manipulate the character into breaking canon or safety rules are flagged.

3. The validated message is routed (520) to a configured LLM to generate a character response.

4. **Post-check** (530): The generated response is evaluated against the Character Card using the full four-dimension evaluation method described in Section C.1.

5. If the response passes (540): The response is transmitted to the user.

6. If the response fails (550): The system either:
   (a) Regenerates the response with stricter prompt parameters (e.g., increased emphasis on the failing dimension), or
   (b) Transmits a predetermined safe fallback response appropriate to the character.

7. The complete interaction is logged (560) with evaluation scores, enabling continuous monitoring and audit.

This real-time mode enables IP owners to deploy AI character experiences with continuous quality assurance, ensuring every user-facing response meets canonical, brand, and legal standards.

#### D. Judge LLM Prompt Engineering

The Judge LLM evaluation prompts are designed with the following principles, each contributing to evaluation accuracy and consistency:

1. **Role Specification**: Each prompt begins by establishing the Judge LLM as an "expert evaluator" for the specific dimension, providing domain context. For example, the canon fidelity prompt establishes the Judge as a "canon fidelity expert evaluator who assesses whether AI-generated character responses align with established canonical facts."

2. **Explicit Scoring Criteria**: Each prompt enumerates 3-4 specific sub-criteria that the Judge must consider. For canon fidelity: factual accuracy, relationship accuracy, lore consistency, absence of hallucination. For voice consistency: personality match, tone appropriateness, vocabulary consistency, speech pattern fidelity. For brand safety: content rating compliance, prohibited topic avoidance, required disclosure inclusion, overall brand protection. For legal compliance: performer consent scope, territory restrictions, usage limitations, required legal notices.

3. **Anchor Points**: Each prompt defines what scores of 0 and 100 mean for the dimension. For example, for canon fidelity: "100 means perfect alignment with all canon facts and relationships; 0 means the response completely contradicts canon or fabricates facts." These anchor points ensure consistent score calibration across evaluations.

4. **Critical Rules**: Certain prompts include bright-line rules to handle clear violations. For brand safety: "Any mention of prohibited topics should result in a score below 50." This prevents the Judge from giving partial credit for responses that contain clear brand safety violations but are otherwise well-written.

5. **Structured Output Format**: All prompts require the Judge to respond in an exact parseable format: `SCORE: [number 0-100]` followed by `EXPLANATION: [2-3 sentences]`. This enables reliable automated parsing of evaluation results.

6. **Low Temperature**: The Judge LLM operates at temperature 0.1 (near-deterministic) to maximize scoring consistency across repeated evaluations of identical inputs. This ensures that the same response evaluated twice receives substantially similar scores.

#### E. Model Agnosticism

A critical feature of the invention is that it is model-agnostic with respect to the AI system that produced the response under evaluation. The method receives only the text output and evaluates it against the Character Card. The underlying model (OpenAI GPT-4, Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, Meta Llama, Mistral, or any other LLM, including future models not yet developed) is irrelevant to the evaluation process.

Referring now to FIG. 6, this model-agnostic architecture is illustrated. Multiple different LLM providers (600a, 600b, 600c, 600d) each generate character responses. These responses are submitted to the Evaluation Service (610), which evaluates each response against the same Character Card (620) using the same four-dimension methodology. The evaluation results (630) are stored and compared regardless of which model produced the response.

This enables:
1. IP owners to switch between LLM providers without recreating evaluation infrastructure
2. Cross-model comparison: running identical test suites against multiple providers to determine which best portrays a given character
3. Future-proofing: new models can be evaluated immediately without system changes
4. Separation of concerns: the evaluation system has no dependency on any specific LLM vendor
5. Marketplace potential: third-party developers can submit their character AI outputs for evaluation and certification by the IP owner

The Judge LLM itself may be any suitable LLM, and may differ from the model that generated the response under evaluation. The choice of Judge LLM is configurable.

#### F. Versioning and Longitudinal Tracking

Character Cards are versioned. Each version is immutable once published. This enables:

1. **Evaluation reproducibility**: A given evaluation can always be re-traced to the exact Character Card version used, ensuring auditability.

2. **Canon evolution tracking**: As a character's canonical facts evolve (e.g., a new season reveals new backstory, a new movie introduces new relationships), new card versions capture the evolution while preserving the complete history. Evaluations run against earlier versions remain valid and comparable within their version context.

3. **Regression detection**: If a previously-passing model begins failing after a model update, the system identifies the regression because evaluations are linked to specific card versions. This enables IP owners to detect when LLM provider updates degrade character portrayal quality.

4. **Approval workflow**: Card versions progress through draft → pending_approval → approved, with multi-stakeholder review. Only approved versions are used for production evaluations, ensuring that evaluation criteria have been reviewed by authorized personnel.

Referring now to FIG. 7, the version lifecycle is shown: A new version is created in "draft" status (700), submitted for review in "pending_approval" status (710), approved and made the current version (720), and eventually archived when a newer version is approved (730). The previous version's evaluation history is preserved.

#### G. Taxonomy and Tag System

The preferred embodiment includes a taxonomy system with categories and tags that standardize character metadata across the platform. This taxonomy enables:

- **Prohibited Content** tags with severity levels (e.g., violence:high, profanity:medium) applied consistently across characters within and across franchises
- **Character Traits** tags (e.g., friendly, loyal, brave) enabling cross-character analytics
- **Content Ratings** (G, PG, PG-13, R) with standardized definitions
- **Relationship Types** (family, friend, romantic, adversary) enabling consistent relationship graph modeling

The taxonomy system supports consistent tagging across characters and franchises, enabling cross-character analytics and standardized safety enforcement at the organization level.

#### H. Multi-Tenant Architecture

The system operates as a multi-tenant cloud service where:

1. Each **Organization** (the IP owner) has isolated data — Character Cards, evaluations, and results from one organization are not visible to or accessible by another organization.

2. **Users** belong to organizations and authenticate via JWT tokens. API requests are automatically scoped to the user's organization.

3. **Franchises** provide a second level of organization within an organization — an IP owner managing multiple properties (e.g., a studio with multiple film franchises) can organize characters by franchise.

4. **Role-based access control** enables different users within an organization to have different permissions (e.g., content creators draft cards, legal reviewers approve legal packs, administrators manage evaluation configurations).

### ABSTRACT

A computer-implemented method for evaluating artificial intelligence (AI) generated text responses that purport to portray a fictional or licensed character. The method comprises: (a) receiving a structured character profile document ("Character Card") containing a canonical facts pack with source citations, a voice/personality profile pack, a legal rights and performer consent pack, and a content safety pack; (b) receiving an AI-generated text response produced by any large language model (LLM) in response to a user prompt directed at the character; (c) independently evaluating the AI-generated response across at least four scoring dimensions — canon fidelity, voice consistency, brand safety, and legal compliance — by submitting the response and the corresponding Character Card pack to a separate adjudicating LLM operating as a judge; (d) computing per-dimension numerical scores on a normalized scale; (e) computing a weighted aggregate score using configurable dimension weights; (f) determining a pass/fail certification outcome by comparing each per-dimension score and the aggregate score against configurable threshold values; and (g) storing the evaluation results, scores, explanations, and pass/fail determination in a persistent data store associated with the character, enabling longitudinal quality tracking across models, versions, and time periods. The method is model-agnostic with respect to the AI system that generated the response under evaluation, enabling intellectual property owners to evaluate outputs from any LLM provider against a single canonical character definition.
